X-Band Antenna System for Device-Free Human Activity Recognition

An ongoing capstone project designing a custom microstrip antenna system to capture the micro-Doppler signatures of human movement — walking, sitting, standing, sleeping — without cameras. The antenna feeds a sensor-fusion pipeline that cross-references RF motion data with a thermal array to filter out non-human movement, classified on a Raspberry Pi 5. Below is the antenna design progression: from a 2.4 GHz proof of concept through to the tuned 10.525 GHz array.

Microstrip Power Divider Feed Networks — ISRO / URSC

During a research internship at the Antenna & Passive Systems Division, U R Rao Satellite Centre (ISRO), I designed and simulated five microstrip power-divider circuits used to distribute RF power across satellite antenna feed networks — progressing from a simple T-junction split to a two-stage Wilkinson divider with unequal-to-equal power ratios. Every design was first verified as an ideal circuit-level schematic, then rebuilt and validated as a full 3D electromagnetic structure in HFSS.

Design 1 — 1×2 T-Junction Power Divider

RT/duroid 6002 · 16.0 × 12.63 × 0.762 mm
−10.65 dB
S11 (HFSS)
−3.5 dB
Insertion loss

The simplest divider topology: a single microstrip line splitting directly into two output branches. It's compact and easy to fabricate, but as a purely reactive network it provides no isolation between its output ports — a reflected signal at one output couples straight into the other. This limitation is exactly what motivates the Wilkinson designs later in the sequence.

Scaling the T-junction to a three-way split. The input match stays strong at −22.41 dB, but isolation between adjacent outputs drops to just −3.10 dB — a large fraction of the power at one output port couples directly into its neighbour through the junction. This is the clearest demonstration in the whole study of why a reactive T-junction can't scale to a real feed network on its own.

The Wilkinson topology solves the T-junction's isolation problem by adding quarter-wave transformer arms and a 100 Ω isolation resistor bridging the outputs. At the ideal circuit level this pushes isolation as high as −46.98 dB; in the full 3D EM simulation it settles at −16.25 dB once fringing fields and layout parasitics are accounted for — still a major improvement over the equivalent T-junction's −3 dB.

The internship's primary design goal, and the most complex circuit in the study: a two-stage cascaded Wilkinson network. The first stage splits the input unequally (resistor R11), and the higher-power branch feeds a second, equal-split Wilkinson stage (R12) — so all three outputs end up receiving an equal one-third share of the input power, while every stage individually retains high isolation.
